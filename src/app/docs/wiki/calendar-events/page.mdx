# Calendar, events & meetings

## What it is

Three related things:

- **Your calendar** — everything you're doing, on a day/week/month grid.
- **Events** — things with a date that other people can register for, buy
  tickets to, and talk about.
- **Meetings** — live video rooms, with recording and speaker-identified
  transcripts.

## How to use your calendar

Open **`/calendar`**. The heading is *My Calendar* and there are four views:
**Day**, **Week** (the default), **Month**, and **Agenda** — the last showing
the next seven days as *Upcoming Events*.

The toolbar has **New Event**, a **Filter** popover, and prev / **Today** / next
navigation.

<Callout type="note" title="Two calendars, one of them better connected">
  `/calendar` has no nav item — it's reachable from the command palette
  (`/` or `Cmd/Ctrl+K` → **Navigate** → *Calendar*) or by typing the address.
  The app's own navigation sends you to your profile's **Calendar** tab
  instead, which is where cross-instance items
  land. In particular, when you claim a job on another instance, that claim is
  projected onto your profile calendar as a green entry linking back to the
  remote job. The Filter popover on `/calendar` currently offers only one
  checkbox, **Events** — shifts and tasks were removed because there's no data
  model behind them yet.
</Callout>

## How to create an event

1. Go to `/create` and pick the **Event** tab (or `/create?tab=event`).
2. Fill in the name and description, and set the date and time with the single
   range selector — a range calendar plus start and end times in one popover.
3. Open the collapsed sections you need: **location or meeting link**, **venue
   booking**, **tickets**, **sessions**. Then hosts, payouts, visibility scope,
   and an image.
4. Publish. Your event gets a page at `/events/[id]`.

## How to run an event

The event page has tabs — **About**, **Attendees**, **Discussion**,
**Announcements**, **Financials** — plus a registration block and a toolbar for
sharing and adding to a calendar.

| You want to… | Go to |
| --- | --- |
| See who's coming | the **Attendees** tab |
| Change details | `/events/[id]/edit` |
| Check money | `/events/[id]/financials` |
| Sell or check tickets | `/events/[id]/tickets` |
| Confirm your own registration | `/events/[id]/registered` |

Attendees who register see a *You're registered!* confirmation page.

<Callout type="note" title="There is no events index">
  `/events` redirects to `/?tab=events`, and the site root ignores `?tab=` — so
  it drops you on the owner's public profile, About tab. That redirect was
  written for the global app's home feed, which a person deploy never renders.
  The listing surfaces that do work here are the **Events** tab on the public
  profile at `/`, `/profile?tab=events` when you're signed in as the owner, and
  `/calendar`. `/events/find-time` does **not** exist — an earlier version of
  this page claimed a scheduling helper that was never in this app.
</Callout>

## How to join a video meeting

Meetings run on LiveKit.

1. Open **`/meetings/[roomName]`**. You get a pre-join screen titled **Join
   Meeting**.
2. Press **Join**. The app requests a token for that room and connects you.
3. Inside you get the video grid, audio and video toggles, screen sharing, a
   chat sidebar, a recording indicator, and a leave button.
4. Use the copy button to share the room link with someone else.

> 📸 **Screenshot:** _A meeting room mid-call — video grid, control bar with mic/camera/screen-share toggles, and the chat sidebar open._ — capture at `/meetings/[roomName]` (join a room). _(placeholder — remove when image added)_

<Callout type="warning" title="You cannot create a meeting from this app's UI">
  The room APIs are complete and working, but **nothing in this app's interface
  calls them**: no button creates a room, no page links to `/meetings/…`, and
  the create-event form's "meeting link" field is free text, not a room creator.
  Today a room name has to come from outside — an API call, or a URL someone
  sends you — and then you visit `/meetings/<roomName>` directly. If LiveKit
  isn't configured on the instance, every meeting route returns 503.
</Callout>

## How transcripts work

Transcription is **not** LiveKit recording. It's a separate loop: your browser
records audio, uploads it, and a Whisper service transcribes it.

1. On an event page, find the transcript panel. (It only renders for events tied
   to a group.)
2. Optionally type a **Speaker label**.
3. Press **Record transcript**. The button becomes **Stop recording**, then the
   status walks through *Uploading audio…* → *Transcribing with WhisperX…* →
   *Transcription complete*.
4. You get timestamped segments with a detected-language badge.
5. Press **Push to KG** to feed the transcript into the knowledge graph.

**Speaker identification:** if you didn't supply a label, the server reads the
diarized segments, counts who spoke most, and attributes the transcript to that
dominant speaker.

The model is two-level: **each contributor has their own personal transcript
document**, and the event carries an **aggregate** assembled from all of them,
with a contributor list.

<Callout type="tip" title="Transcribing something that isn't an event">
  `POST /api/transcription` does the same job for any audio and files the result
  as a document in your faceted vault — you can give it a title and a facet
  path. See [Docs & vault](/docs/wiki/docs-vault).
</Callout>

## Good to know

- **"Virtual" is guessed, not declared.** An event is treated as virtual when
  its location text contains *zoom*, *virtual*, or *online* — **or when it has
  no location at all**. There's no flag, so an event you simply never gave a
  venue reads as virtual.
- **Live invitations** are a different thing: a post marked as a live invitation
  shares your location for one hour and surfaces through
  `GET /api/live-invitations`. See [Feeds & posts](/docs/wiki/feeds-posts).
- Event pages are public; registering, buying tickets, and editing are not.

**API**

- `GET`/`POST /api/meetings` — list rooms (`mtg-` prefix), create a room + host token
- `POST /api/meetings/[roomName]/token` — a join token
- `GET`/`POST`/`DELETE /api/meetings/[roomName]/livestream` — RTMP egress
- `GET`/`POST /api/events/[id]/meeting` — create or read the room bound to an event
- `GET`/`POST /api/events/[id]/transcribe` — personal + aggregate transcripts
- `POST /api/transcription` — generic audio → vault document
- `GET /api/live-invitations`

**Configuration**

- `LIVEKIT_URL` / `LIVEKIT_WS_URL`, `LIVEKIT_API_KEY`, `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET`
- `WHISPER_TRANSCRIBE_URL` (+ key and model), or `OPENAI_API_KEY` with
  `OPENAI_TRANSCRIPTION_MODEL`; a local Whisper fallback exists server-side
- Room prefixes: `mtg-` meetings, `evt-` event rooms, `live-` livestreams

## Adding a local admission tax (organizer-remitted)

Some cities levy admission taxes that fall on the ORGANIZER — Denver's 10%
Facilities Development Admission Tax is the classic example — and marketplace
rules don't shift them to RIVR. If your event owes one, enable **Add a local
admission tax** on the create-event form: give it the name buyers should see,
your chosen percentage, and your tax registration ID (required — no
registration, no tax). It appears as its own separately-stated line at
checkout, is computed on the ticket subtotal, is excluded from RIVR's platform
fee, and is paid out **to you** with your ticket revenue. **You are
responsible for remitting it to your taxing authority; RIVR does not remit
it.** One organizer tax per event; changes never affect completed orders.

## Venue address & ticket tax

An in-person event needs a structured **venue address** (street, city, state,
ZIP — entered on the create-event form) before paid tickets can be sold:
admissions tax is calculated where the event takes place, not where the buyer
lives. Ticket checkout for paid in-person events refuses until the organizer
adds one. Online events don't need a venue address — their tickets are taxed
at the buyer's address like any digital purchase.
