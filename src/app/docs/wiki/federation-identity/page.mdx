# Federation & identity

## What it is

You are one person across many instances. This one is your **home** — it is
authoritative for who you are — and it federates outward to the global
aggregator and to peer instances so your identity, content and memberships
travel with you.

Three ideas carry most of the weight:

- **Your home instance owns your identity.** Other instances hold projections of
  it, not the original.
- **Nothing is trusted because it says so.** Every cross-instance claim is either
  a session you hold or a signature this instance can verify against a registered
  peer's public key. An asserted node id is never enough.
- **You can visit other instances, and they can visit you** — under a capability
  scope the host controls.

## How signing in across instances works

1. From another instance, you start at
   `/api/federation/sso/start?homeBaseUrl=…`.
2. You're redirected to **your home** instance, where you're already signed in.
   It mints a signed assertion for you.
3. You're bounced back and land at `/api/federation/sso/land`, which **verifies
   the signature locally** against the registered peer's public key — no callback
   to the issuer — and gives you a browsing session.

While browsing that way, the app can tell you whose instance you're on and who
you're signed in as.

When someone lands on **your** instance this way, what they can do is governed by
your visitor policy — see [Instance owner](/docs/wiki/admin).

## How to look after your recovery key

Your sovereign identity is backed by a BIP-39 seed phrase.

- **At signup**, the phrase is generated **in your browser**. You confirm you've
  written it down and type back one of its words. Only a public key and a
  fingerprint are sent to the server.
- **Later**, Settings → **Security** lets you reveal or rotate it, each behind an
  emailed challenge code and your device passphrase.

The server never sees your mnemonic or private key — at signup or ever.

Rotation is transactional: the old key is archived, your credential version is
bumped, and a signed rotation event is queued for the rest of the federation.

If you're locked out entirely, recovery is driven from the global instance,
which issues a signed assertion your home instance redeems. That path is
replay-protected — a re-sent assertion is refused with a conflict rather than
being applied twice.

## How to move to a sovereign home

If you started on the hosted global app and now run your own instance, the
sovereign merge connects the two.

1. On global, you enter your sovereign instance's address.
2. A window opens **on your instance** at `/sovereign-merge-confirm`, showing
   exactly which global instance is asking.
3. If you're not signed in here, you're asked to sign in first — with your
   approval request preserved.
4. You approve, and you're returned to global.

<Callout type="warning" title="Not production-ready">
  The connect-to-sovereign flow still needs manual intervention for new users.
  Treat it as a supported-with-help path, not a self-service one.
</Callout>

## What is public about your instance

Some federation endpoints are deliberately open, because federation can't
bootstrap otherwise:

| Endpoint | What it exposes |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /api/federation/registry` | Registered instances plus this one's slug, base URL, **public key**, and primary agent — this is how another instance learns the key to verify your signatures |
| `GET /api/federation/registry/[agentId]` | Which instance is authoritative for an agent |
| `GET /api/federation/manifest` | This instance's signed Universal Manifest |
| `GET /api/federation/node-status` | A read-only node probe, used by the composer to show your federation reach |
| `GET /api/universal-manifest/[kind]/[id]` | A manifest for a person, organization, project, post, event, or offer |
| `GET /api/murmurations/profiles/[kind]/[id]` | A Murmurations-format profile |

Publishing to the Murmurations network index is **opt-in**, from Settings →
Notifications.

## Machine-to-machine

These are peer-authenticated and never involve a user click: peer registration
(`/api/federation/peers`), the event feed peers poll (`/api/federation/events`),
event import and export, cross-instance mutations
(`/api/federation/mutations` — where a visitor's action is checked against their
granted capability), agent assertions, remote assertion verification, and
delegated password verification.

Delivery and catch-up run on a schedule: one job pushes queued local events out
to peers, another pulls their feeds in and projects them locally. The pull side
can catch up on more than a week of downtime, with signature and replay checks
still applied.

## Good to know

- **Content federates in both directions.** Posts you write project outward as
  signed events; group memberships you hold elsewhere are projected back onto
  your profile's Groups tab.
- **Links to remote things go to their real home.** A group, ring, family or
  project that lives on another instance links to that instance — this app has
  no local route for them and won't pretend otherwise.
- **A federated visitor is not you.** Everything under `/settings`, `/autobot`,
  `/builder`, `/api/admin`, `/api/personas` and friends is refused to anyone who
  isn't the instance owner, at the edge.
- **Anonymous viewers see only your public subset**, filtered server-side before
  any page limit is applied.
- Published builder sites are served by hostname *before* the auth gate, so a
  site on your own domain is public even though the instance behind it isn't.
