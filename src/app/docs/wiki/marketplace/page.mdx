# Marketplace

## What it is

The marketplace is where offerings — products, services, bookable time — are
listed and bought. On this sovereign person instance it plays **two roles at
once**, and it helps to keep them apart:

- **You as buyer.** You open a listing, pick a payment method, see exactly what
  you will be charged, and pay. Card, wallet balance, and (for crypto-priced
  listings) one-signature USDC all land here.
- **You as seller.** Your own offerings live on your profile's **Offerings**
  tab. Before anyone can pay you by card you must finish Stripe Connect
  onboarding — until then card checkout on your listings is refused with a
  reason.

There is no marketplace *index* on this instance. A person instance hosts one
person, so browsing a catalogue happens on the global aggregator; individual
listings render here at `/marketplace/[id]`.

<Callout type="warning" title="The /marketplace URL does not take you anywhere useful">
  It redirects to `/?tab=marketplace`, and the site root ignores `?tab=` — so
  you land on the owner's public profile, About tab. That redirect was written
  for the global app's Mart tab, which a person deploy never renders.
</Callout>

The links that actually reach a listing on this instance are:

- a **post with an embedded offering** — in the profile Posts tab or at
  `/posts/[id]`, its buy affordance goes to `/marketplace/[id]`
- your profile's **Wallet → Sales** rows, each with an **Open** button
- **service entries on the profile Calendar** tab
- the **relationship graph** on the profile About tab
- a direct link, or the listing's canonical URL on the global aggregator

## How to buy something

1. Open a listing at `/marketplace/[id]`. You'll see the offering, the seller,
   quantity, and — if the seller published a bookable schedule — a slot picker.
2. Press the primary action (its label follows the listing type — e.g. *Buy*,
   *Book*, *Claim Voucher*). You land on `/marketplace/[id]/purchase`.
3. Choose a **payment method**. The selector shows up to three:
   - **Card** — always offered. Stripe hosted checkout. If the seller hasn't
     enabled card payments you get their reason inline instead of a button.
   - **Rivr Wallet** — shown only when you're signed in **and** your balance is
     above zero. If the balance is under the total, checkout stays disabled and
     the error names both numbers.
   - **Crypto Wallet** — shown only when the seller priced the listing in `USDC`
     or `ETH`; USDC listings carry the sub-label *"Pay with USDC on Base"*. If
     you have no crypto address on file the row appears but is disabled.
4. Read the **Order Summary**. It shows the line item, a single **Platform fee**
   row, and the **Total**. The fee row changes with your payment method — see
   [What you actually pay](#what-you-actually-pay) below.

5. Press checkout. Card sends you to Stripe; wallet settles in-app; crypto asks
   your wallet for **one** signature.
6. You land on **Purchase Successful!** at `/marketplace/[id]/confirmed`, with an

7. Your itemised receipt lives at `/marketplace/[id]/receipt/[receiptId]` —
   **Date**, an optional **Booked Window**, then **Subtotal**, **Fees**, and
   **Total**, plus the payment id and email. If the purchase is completed and
   has a Stripe payment intent, a **Request Refund** button appears; the state
   then moves to *Refund Requested* and finally *Refunded*.

<Callout type="warning" title="The confirmation page's order number is cosmetic">
  It is built as `ORD-` plus the first eight characters of the **listing** id,
  and the date shown is the moment the page rendered — not the purchase
  timestamp. Use the receipt page (and its **Payment ID** row) as the
  authoritative record.
</Callout>

### Buying without an account

You can start a purchase signed out. At checkout you're offered a choice: sign
in, or continue as a guest. **Guest always routes to card** — Stripe handles
guest identity — because wallet and crypto need an account to settle against.

## What you actually pay

RIVR quotes a **seller-net** price: the seller receives the number on the
listing, and everything else is grossed up onto the buyer total. One unified
margin applies to every transaction type on the fleet.

| Component | Value | Constant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RIVR margin (%) | **3.30%** of the seller price | `MARKETPLACE_FEE_BPS = 330` |
| RIVR margin (flat) | **$1.49** | `PLATFORM_MARGIN_FIXED_CENTS = 149` |
| Card processing | 2.9% + 30¢, grossed up so it lands on the buyer | `STRIPE_CARD_PERCENT_BPS`, `STRIPE_CARD_FIXED_CENTS` |
| Connect account overhead | 5% of the seller price, **capped at $2.00** | `CONNECT_OVERHEAD_RECOVERY_BPS = 500` |

That overhead **taper** is the part worth understanding: Stripe's ~$2/month
active-account cost used to be recovered as a flat $2 on every purchase, which
made small carts absurd (a $6 item carried a $2.86 buyer fee). It now scales at
5% and caps at $2, so a $6 cart recovers 30¢ while **every cart at or above $40
is priced exactly as before**.

The paths differ in what the summary shows you:

- **Card** — the full grossed-up difference between the seller's price and your
  total (`buyerPlatformFeeCents`). Everything in the table above is inside it.
- **Wallet** — the RIVR margin only (`platformFeeCents`), and **only on
  `product` listings**; wallet purchases of anything else show no platform fee
  at all. There's no card network to pay, so there's no processing gross-up.
- **Crypto** — a different formula again: a flat **3.3% and nothing else**. No
  $1.49, no Connect overhead, no card gross-up. The relayer's gas is treated as
  the platform's cost of goods.

<Callout type="note" title="The buyer breakdown is deliberately coarse">
  You see two money lines — *Platform fee* and *Total* — not five. Stripe
  processing, Connect overhead, org commission and referral splits all exist in
  `CheckoutFeeResult` and travel in the Stripe session metadata, but none of
  them is itemised in the buyer UI. The receipt is similarly coarse: a single
  **Fees** line.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A per-instance fee override can make the preview differ from the charge">
  The purchase page computes its preview with the default 3.3% + $1.49. The
  server route resolves a `marketplaceFeePolicy` first (a per-listing,
  owner-group or instance-group override, clamped to 0–5000 bps) and charges
  with that. When a non-default policy applies, the displayed fee and the
  charged fee can disagree.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="Cross-border corridors are priced elsewhere">
  The shared fee engine (`src/lib/checkout-fees.ts`) understands three seller
  payout corridors — `domestic`, `connect_cross_border` (0.25% + 25¢) and
  `global_payouts` ($1.50 flat + 1.25% volume + 1% FX) — and grosses them onto
  the buyer so the seller still nets face value. **On this instance nothing
  passes a corridor**: the purchase page calls `calculateCheckoutFees` with no
  `payoutCorridor`, so it defaults to `domestic` and no surcharge is added.
  Corridor pricing is applied by the instance that knows the seller's payout
  country — the seller's own home, or global. There is no payout-country picker
  here, because a person instance is buyer-side for other people's listings.
</Callout>

## How to pay in USDC

The crypto lane is a **one-signature** checkout — you never send a transaction
yourself, and you never approve a spender.

1. On a listing priced in `USDC`, pick **Crypto Wallet**.
2. The app asks `POST /api/crypto/checkout` for a `quote`. The server computes
   the split — price, platform fee, seller address — entirely from the listing.
   **The client never authors amounts or recipients.**
3. You sign the returned EIP-3009 typed data. A salt binds your signature to
   that exact split. **The quote is valid for 15 minutes.**
4. The app posts `submit`. The server **recomputes the same split and rejects
   any drift**, then relays your signed authorization through the RivrSplitter
   contract. On-chain success writes a receipt and a ledger row.

**Networks:** Base mainnet and Base Sepolia only, chosen server-side from
`CRYPTO_NETWORK`.

<Callout type="warning" title="USDC only, despite what the selector offers">
  The payment selector shows the crypto option for listings priced in `USDC`
  **or** `ETH`, but `/api/crypto/checkout` serves USDC only — an ETH-priced
  listing is rejected with *"Listing not found, not USDC-priced, or seller has
  no crypto address."* One-signature mode also requires the splitter to be
  enabled; otherwise the client falls back to a two-transfer MetaMask flow.
</Callout>

## How to sell

1. Go to `/profile?tab=wallet`. Before you have a payout account the Wallet tab
   shows a **Become a Seller** card. Press **Set Up Payout Account**.
2. A dialog asks **"Where is your bank?"** Pick the country of the bank account
   you want to be paid into, from a 22-country shortlist (United States,
   Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Canada, Costa Rica, Denmark, Finland,
   France, Germany, India, Ireland, Italy, Mexico, Netherlands, Norway,
   Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom). A note under the
   picker tells you which payout rail that country uses. Press **Continue**.
3. Complete Stripe's hosted Express onboarding.
4. Back on the Wallet tab, check the status rows — **Details submitted**,
   **Charges enabled**, **Payouts enabled**. If you left onboarding
   half-finished the button reads **Complete Setup**.
5. Create the offering from `/create`. It appears on your profile's
   **Offerings** tab.
6. Sales show up under your profile's **Wallet** tab → **Sales** sub-view,
   alongside your available and pending Stripe balances.

<Callout type="warning" title="Start from the Wallet tab, not Settings">
  `/settings` has a **Seller** tab with a **Stripe USD Wallet** card and a
  **Set Up Stripe USD Wallet** button. It creates the same connected account —
  but it never asks for your bank country, so it defaults to the United
  States. **The country of a Stripe account cannot be changed afterwards.** If
  your bank is anywhere other than the US, start from the profile Wallet tab's
  **Become a Seller** card, which asks. Once an account exists both surfaces
  show the same status rows and the same Complete Setup button; the country
  question is only asked for a brand-new account.
</Callout>

  Until Connect onboarding is complete, `listing.cardCheckoutAvailable` is
  false and buyers see your reason string instead of a card button. Wallet and
  crypto paths are unaffected.
</Callout>

## Good to know

- **Buyer identity is always server-derived.** Card, wallet and crypto all key
  the purchase to the session principal (or, for a federated visitor, the
  unified-session principal with first-contact projection). Client-supplied
  buyer metadata is never trusted.
- **You cannot buy your own listing** — the server refuses it.
- **Bookable listings block checkout until you pick a valid slot.**
- **Quantity is capped** by the listing's available stock; when it reaches zero
  the checkout button disables.
- Checkout is rate-limited per client IP (marketplace checkout is IP-keyed
  rather than user-keyed precisely so guests can buy).
- **Federated listings bounce home.** Opening a mirrored listing redirects you
  to the instance that actually owns it.
- Card sales settle into RIVR-managed capital accounts; Stripe Connect is the
  payout rail, not the ledger.
- Membership products are sold on their own pages — see
  [Wallet & money](/docs/wiki/wallet-treasury).

## Sales tax

Where RIVR (the platform) holds an active tax registration, checkout adds
sales tax ON TOP of the listed price — computed from the buyer's address for
products, and from the venue for in-person event tickets. The seller's net is
never reduced by tax, and the collected tax is remitted by RIVR, not by you.
Wallet balance cannot collect sales tax, so taxable items must be paid by
card once RIVR is registered. Full picture:
[Getting paid & taxes](/docs/wiki/getting-paid-taxes).
