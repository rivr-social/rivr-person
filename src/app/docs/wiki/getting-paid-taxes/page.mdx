# Getting paid & taxes

## What it is

The tax picture for you as the instance owner (and anyone you sell to or pay), in plain language. RIVR runs on ONE Stripe
platform (the global instance); this personal instance holds Stripe **Connect** accounts
for payouts, and card checkouts are (or are becoming) **Global-mediated** —
created on the global platform, settled back to this instance's ledger
identically to a local sale. There are three separate tax layers, and it
matters who owes what:

| Layer | Who owes it | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sales tax at checkout** | The buyer pays it; RIVR (the platform) collects and remits it as a marketplace facilitator, wherever RIVR holds a tax registration | Automatic — computed at checkout, added ON TOP of your price; your net is never reduced |
| **Local admission taxes** (e.g. a city facilities/amusement tax) | The EVENT ORGANIZER | You declare it on your event (name + percentage); it's charged as its own line and **paid out to you** — you remit it |
| **Income-tax paperwork** (W-9 / W-8BEN, 1099-K) | The seller/payee | Collected at payout setup; RIVR issues US sellers a 1099-K when thresholds are met |

## Sales tax at checkout

Where RIVR holds an active registration, checkout calculates sales tax from
the right source and adds it on top of the listed price:

- **Products** are taxed at the buyer's address.
- **In-person event tickets** are taxed where the EVENT takes place — which
  is why an in-person event needs a **venue address** before paid tickets can
  be sold. Online events are taxed at the buyer's address.
- The collected tax is remitted by RIVR and is **never part of your payout or
  your reported sales** — your net is the listed price.
- **Wallet balance cannot collect sales tax**, so taxable items (tangible
  goods, in-person admissions) must be paid by card once RIVR is registered.
  Services remain wallet-payable.

## Your local admission tax (organizer-remitted)

If your city levies an admission tax that falls on YOU as the organizer,
enable **Add a local admission tax** on the create-event form — a name buyers
see, your chosen percentage, and (optionally) your registration ID for your
own records. It appears as its own separately-stated line, is part of the
charged total (RIVR's platform fee and processing gross-up apply to the full
amount charged), and is **paid out to you** with your ticket revenue. **You
remit it; RIVR does not.** Details:
[Calendar & events](/docs/wiki/calendar-events).

## Your tax identity (payout setup)

Payout setup runs through Stripe's hosted onboarding on the global platform:

- **US sellers** provide their tax identity (SSN/EIN) there — the W-9
  equivalent, verified by Stripe. Until it verifies, payouts can be held.
  RIVR — not Stripe — issues your 1099-K when federal/state thresholds are
  met; collected sales tax is excluded from that gross.
- **Non-US sellers** provide a W-8BEN instead; most foreign payouts withhold
  0% because the work isn't US-source, and any withholding that does apply is
  shown before you're paid.

## Refunds and tax

A refunded sale reverses its tax too: the buyer gets the tax back and the
collected amount is removed from RIVR's remittance records. Your own
recovered net follows the refund policy on
[Wallet & treasury](/docs/wiki/wallet-treasury).

## Good to know

- Prices on RIVR are always **tax-exclusive** — tax is added at checkout and
  stated separately, never carved out of your listed price.
- RIVR's platform fee is computed on the full amount charged (including an
  organizer admission tax line), and Stripe's processing cost is grossed into
  the buyer total — the buyer funds the fees, you net your price.
- Nothing here is tax advice; what you owe on YOUR income and YOUR
  organizer-remitted taxes is between you and your taxing authority.
