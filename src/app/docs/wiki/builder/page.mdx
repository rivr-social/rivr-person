# Builder

## What it is

An AI site builder that turns a description into a working website, then puts it
on the internet — optionally on your own domain, served straight from this
instance.

It does three increasingly ambitious things:

1. **Build a site.** Describe what you want; the assistant writes the files, and
   you preview them live.
2. **Publish it.** One button, and it's served — on this instance's hostname, or
   a domain you own.
3. **Run whole applications.** Beyond static sites, the **Apps** panel deploys
   real containerised apps on your own server, each with its own subdomain and
   optional database.

## How to build a site

1. Go to **`/builder`** — *AI Site Builder*, "Describe your site and watch it
   come to life."
2. Pick a workspace from the select at the top. **Default (sovereign site)** is
   your main one.
3. Describe your site in the chat pane on the left. Files stream back and appear
   in the right panel.
4. Switch the right panel between its six views: **Preview**, **Files**,
   **Data**, **History**, **Deploy**, and **Apps**.

The site can be seeded from your own profile data rather than from nothing — the
**Data** view binds sources like your profile, a Universal Manifest, or a Solid
Pod, and the generator builds a site around them.

## How to edit with the assistant

The **Deploy** view carries an assistant panel that edits your workspace
directly, rather than streaming whole files at you. Ask it to change something
and it lists, reads, writes and deletes files inside the workspace.

It is **jailed to that workspace** — a path jail with an extension allowlist,
a 400 KB per-file limit, a 2 MB workspace limit, a 60-file cap, and an
undeletable `index.html`. It can publish, but only through the very same
owner-gated publish service the Deploy button uses, and its instructions forbid
publishing unless you explicitly ask.

Changed files appear as chips so you can see what it touched before you ship.

## How to publish

1. Open the **Deploy** view and press publish.
2. Your files are snapshotted as a version *and* written to object storage, and a
   publication record is created.
3. The site is served immediately on this instance's hostname.

Every publish is a version. **History** lists your last 50 snapshots and you can
restore any of them.

## How to use your own domain

In the Deploy view's custom-domain panel:

1. Enter your domain. The panel shows the **A/CNAME records** you need.
2. Add them at your registrar — or press **Set DNS for me** if you've connected
   Cloudflare, Namecheap, or Squarespace, and the app writes them for you.
3. Press **Verify** — a real DNS lookup against this instance's host.
4. Press **Bind**.

After binding, any request arriving with that hostname is served your published
site directly from this instance.

<Callout type="note" title="Your DNS credentials never leave the server">
  The one-click DNS write decrypts your stored connector credential server-side.
  The browser never holds it, and the app never accepts DNS credentials from the
  client.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="Two different domain features">
  This binds a domain to a **published site**. The Federation tab in
  [Settings](/docs/wiki/account-settings) points a domain at **the whole
  instance**. They're separate.
</Callout>

## How to run a full application

The **Apps** panel manages real applications on your own server — each in its own
container, on its own subdomain.

1. Open the **Apps** view and press **Create New App Workspace**. Give it a name
   and pick a template.
2. Build the app in its workspace.
3. Drive it through its lifecycle: **deploy**, **stop**, **start**, **rollback**,
   **archive**, **delete**.
4. Bind a custom domain to it from the same panel — verify, then bind.

Every app is described by a typed manifest: an app id, a runtime (`static` or
`node-22`), a resource class, an optional Postgres database, the *names* of its
secrets, and a health path. Status refreshes every few seconds and shows the real
phase: *queued, validating, building, deploying, running, stopped, archived,
deleted,* or *failed*.

You can also ship a **builder site** as its own app — its own container and
hostname instead of the shared serve path — from the "Own environment" panel.

<Callout type="warning" title="Deleting an app takes two steps on purpose">
  An app must be **archived first**, and then you must confirm by echoing its
  app id back. There is no single-click delete.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="What the browser is never allowed to send">
  Your session never supplies Docker, Compose, or host-path material — only the
  typed manifest and a lifecycle action. Requests are written to a spool that a
  host-side broker validates and executes independently, and the broker
  re-validates the manifest itself. Builds run in a resource-capped container;
  apps run with hard CPU, memory, process and log limits; releases are retained
  so rollback works. Domain bindings are re-verified by the broker before it
  routes traffic, so a stale DNS entry can't capture someone else's hostname.
</Callout>

## Good to know

- **The builder is owner-only**, enforced at the edge and re-checked in the
  routes themselves. Site publications are home-authority and never federated.
- **Data scope can only narrow, never widen.** Anything the built site reads
  through the runtime data endpoint is first bounded by your own view
  permissions, and leaving a scope empty is *not* a wildcard.
- **Reading your live site back** into the builder is possible, subject to an
  extension allowlist and per-file size caps.
- The builder can also connect a **GitHub repo** and deploy there, and can
  **import a Solid Pod** profile.
- Builder **tables** give you lightweight structured data for your site, scoped
  to you — a table belonging to anyone else is simply reported as missing.
- `/builder` is linked from one place: the **Control Plane** card on your
  profile.

### Builder vs bespoke

You'll see both names in the API. **Bespoke** is the deterministic generator
underneath — it turns a profile module bundle into site files. **Builder** is
everything around it: the workspace, the chat, versioning, publishing, domains,
and app lifecycle. The builder calls the bespoke generator to seed a new site.

## Under the hood

- `/builder` · `src/app/(main)/builder/page.tsx` (the whole builder is in this
  one client component — there is no `builder-client.tsx`)
- `src/components/builder-apps-panel.tsx`, `builder-assistant-panel.tsx`,
  `custom-domain-panel.tsx`, `site-environment-panel.tsx`
- `src/lib/builder/**` — `app-manifest`, `app-lifecycle`, `app-domains`,
  `app-templates`, `assistant-tools`, `dns-write`, `site-app-bridge`,
  `site-host-resolve`, `site-owner`, `site-publications`,
  `site-publish-storage`, `tables`
- `src/lib/bespoke/**` — the generator, system prompts, data-source registry
- Host broker · `tools/camalot/rivr-app-broker.py`
- Serving · `src/middleware.ts` host-dispatch →
  `src/app/site-host/[[...path]]/route.ts`

**API** — `/api/builder/`: `apps`, `apps/[appId]`, `apps/[appId]/domains`,
`apps/[appId]/lifecycle`, `assistant`, `chat`, `data-sources`, `deploy`,
`domain`, `github-connection`, `github-files`, `import-solid`, `live-files`,
`publish`, `rea-source`, `site-app`, `tables`, `tables/[tableId]/rows`,
`versions`, `versions/[id]/restore`, `workspace-deploy`, `workspace-files`.
Plus `/api/bespoke/{generate,generate-site,deploy,deploy-files}`.

<Callout type="warning" title="There is no /api/builder/site">
  Publishing goes through `/api/builder/publish`; the own-environment deploy goes
  through `/api/builder/site-app`. An earlier version of this page cited
  `/api/builder/site`, which does not exist.
</Callout>
